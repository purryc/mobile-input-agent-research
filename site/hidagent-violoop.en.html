HIDAgent × VIOLOOP

Shared external eyes and hands; different system boundaries. HIDAgent is a cross-device research bridge. VIOLOOP packages this approach as a desktop work agent.

VIOLOOP here means the Bvio Tech hardware product. Sending keystrokes does not establish correct intent, permission or task reliability.

02 / What the paper establishes

Jeffrey P. Bigham separates observation and execution from the decision model. A Pico emulates keyboard/mouse input; serial communication connects the controller; HDMI capture supplies pixels. Six prototypes cover UI agents, UI datasets, screen readers, cross-device interaction, screen assistance and social-media collection. Their evaluation is descriptive, not proof of robust complex-task completion.

100 trials: M3 Mac controller and iPhone 17 target. These are not end-to-end task times and cannot be compared directly with TOPS or token throughput. Paper §4

The paper calibrates screen coordinates against relative pointer motion. The current repository instead describes visual closed-loop steering, cached mappings and reconnect detection. Reproduce the paper and current implementation as different versions. Current README/code

No target agent does not mean zero setup: iOS requires accessibility settings; Android requires video-mirroring permission. A phone simultaneously handling capture and HID communication remains unresolved in the paper. Screen-change detection is only an initial takeover heuristic. Paper §3, §5–6

03 / Product evidence boundaries

VIOLOOP promotes local models, hardware approval and cross-app operation. Its throughput and security statements are vendor claims, not independently verified here. Official site

The FAQ describes server-relayed BYOK, unlike the homepage’s direct-provider claim. It marks the campaign ended and shipping estimated, not verified delivery or fully offline operation. FAQ · Homepage

04 / Implications for Mobile Input Agent

Design proposals below, not demonstrated product capabilities.

  1. Separate layers: phone expression, pointing and parameter editing; decision and permission checks; execution through API, accessibility or HID. A bridge is not a decision model.
  2. Bind approval: show target device, object, parameters and expected changes; expire grants. Revalidate stale context rather than treat one press as unlimited authority.
  3. Explicit takeover: distinguish touch-to-pause, release-to-stop, undo and resume. Screen change alone cannot infer takeover.
  4. Measure whole tasks: compare success, misclicks, stale context, device switching, recovery and p95 latency across channels, not just inference speed.

A useful combination: HIDAgent-style experimental coverage and a visible approval control, with independent context-versioning, permission and execution-receipt protocols. See protocol and decision models.
